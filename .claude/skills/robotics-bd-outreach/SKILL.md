# Robotics BD Outreach

Personal skill for Pete Dormer. Writes the first-touch BD message to a robotics or autonomy target so it reads like a specialist who actually knows their world got in touch, not like a recruiter running a template through an AI.

This is for opening a relationship: cold or warm first contact with a founder, engineer, engineering leader, or talent team. It is not for the anonymous candidate-spec send (that leads with the candidate's solved problem and keeps its own established rules).

## The one rule everything else serves

The message must never read as AI written. A prospect (Julian at Bliq) once replied calling Pete's outreach "AI slop" after a detector scored it 94% AI. That message had been drafted straight off the advert. Never do that again.

Defer to the `humanizer` skill for the mechanics of removing AI tells (inflated significance, promotional adjectives, superficial -ing clauses, rule-of-three, negative parallelism, uniform sentence rhythm). Do not duplicate that list here. This skill adds the BD-specific tells humanizer does not cover, in the Bans section below. Run the humanizer self-audit ("what makes this obviously AI generated?") on every draft before it goes out.

## Inputs to gather first

Before drafting, know three things. If any are missing, ask, do not guess:

1. Who the recipient is and their seat: founder/CEO, engineer/IC, engineering leader (CTO, VP Eng, Head of Robotics), or talent/TA. This decides the whole shape. See `reference/position-playbook.md`.
2. One genuine, specific thing about their product or company that Pete can honestly say he rates. Not the headline, the detail underneath it. This is the opener.
3. Whether there is a live role in play, and if several, which is the hard one (the specialist corner, the long-open seat).

Fact-check any claim about their funding, product, or people before it goes in. A wrong "congratulations on the Series B" is worse than no opener.

## Shape of a good message

Principles, not a script. Vary the order and the structure every time, or a run of these starts to read templated again.

- Open with the specific thing from input 2, said plainly. One real observation that proves Pete looked. Never oversell it, no "groundbreaking", "world-leading", "incredible". If it needs an adjective to sound impressive, it is the wrong detail.
- Make it unambiguous, early, that Pete is a recruiter who specialises in robotics and autonomy. Not coy, not disguised as a peer. The specialism is the credibility.
- Name-drop one relevant client Pete has helped, chosen by fit: same vertical, adjacent tech, or a recognisable local/regional name. Check `akkar-client-list` first for who is a client and who is off-limits. One name, worked into a sentence, never a logo list.
- Position the coverage: Pete's desk covers the whole robotics and autonomy stack, so he is one partner for any of it, perception, controls, embedded, ROS2, navigation, simulation. This is "full stack across the domain", not full-stack software engineering. Make that unmistakable.
- If there is a live hard role, lean into it: name it as the one Pete would most want to talk about because he knows that part of the market. See the difficult-role rule below.
- Close softly, on their side: an ask to hear about the business plans and the role, with a light diary prompt. Never assumptive, never about Pete's career.
- Pete's booking link at the foot: https://scheduler.zoom.us/akkar-petedormer-vwg/30-mins-with-pete

Length: short and scannable, one screen on a phone. Roughly 90 to 160 words. A founder message sits at the short end, they have the least time. An engineer message can run a little longer if the technical hook earns it.

## The difficult-role rule

Lean into a live hard role by naming it as the one Pete wants to talk about, framed as his read of the market: "the [role] is the one I'd want to start on, that corner of the market is thin and I spend most of my time in it." That signals competence.

Never frame it as their problem. Banned: "this role must be hard to fill", "I know how tough this talent pool is", "rare skill set", "I appreciate how difficult this market is for you". Telling a hiring manager their own role is hard reads as a machine guessing at their pain. It is the single fastest way to sound like slop.

## Bans (BD-specific, on top of humanizer)

- Never recite their advert's or JD's tech stack back at them. They wrote it. Repeating it proves nothing except that a template read the advert.
- Never explain their own product, market, or technology to the people who built it. No paragraph about what their company does or why their field matters.
- The retired template is dead: congratulate on a milestone, sympathise about how hard the role is, pivot to Pete's relevance, soft close. Do not reproduce that arc in any wording.
- No "I hope this finds you well", no throat-clearing, no run-up. The point lands in the first line.
- Never lead with Pete or make the message about him. One line of who he is, no career story.
- Do not mention Cubiq unless Pete raises it first.

## Before it goes out

Run the `humanizer` audit, then a quick pass/fail gate (see `quality-rubrics` for a formal version): does the opener prove he looked, is it obviously a specialist recruiter, is there exactly one fitting client name-drop, is the close on their side, is there nothing in it they could have got from their own advert, and does it read differently from the last message sent to this kind of seat. Any no, fix before sending.

## Gotchas

- **Fixing the tells but leaving four identical mid-length sentences.** Uniform rhythm is itself an AI signature. Vary sentence length hard: a three-word line next to a long one.
- **A "specific" opener that is actually generic.** "I love what you're building in autonomous driving" is not specific, it fits a hundred companies. If the opener would fit a competitor, it fails. Go one level deeper into an actual choice they made.
- **Name-dropping a company that is off-limits or not a client.** Always check `akkar-client-list`. Dropping a name Pete cannot back up is worse than dropping none.
- **Same structure every message.** Even a clean, human message becomes a template if the tenth one opens the same way as the first. Move the pieces around. Vary which element leads.
- **Position-matching collapsing to one voice.** If the founder draft and the engineer draft could swap recipients unchanged, the matching has not happened. The engineer wants the problem, the founder wants the business, the TA wants a partner who gets the domain. Different centres of gravity.
- **Over-reciting the difficult role into their pain.** Leaning in is about Pete's market knowledge, never their struggle. Re-read the difficult-role rule if a line starts with "I know how hard".
- **Treating full-stack as full-stack software.** It means whole-of-domain coverage in robotics and autonomy. If the sentence could be read as offering web developers, rewrite it.
