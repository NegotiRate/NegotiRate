# Position playbook

The recipient's seat decides the centre of gravity. Match it or the message reads generic. The through-line stays the same in all four: specific product hook, clearly a robotics/autonomy specialist recruiter, one fitting client name-drop, whole-stack coverage, soft close asking about their plans, diary link. What changes is what the message is *about*.

## Founder / CEO / co-founder

Time-poor, thinking about the business and momentum, treats hiring as a growth lever. Shortest message of the four.

- Centre of gravity: the business bet, not the tech internals. The hook should show Pete understands what they are building commercially and why the bet is interesting.
- Value to them: a specialist who can move on the hires the roadmap depends on, without them having to manage a generalist agency.
- Close on the business: ask to hear where they are taking it and what the near-term hiring looks like.
- Avoid: talking to them like an engineer, middleware detail, explaining their own market back to them.

## Engineer / IC (often the hiring manager on a small team)

Allergic to recruiter spam and to anyone who does not get the domain. This is the audience most likely to run a detector or forward it as a joke.

- Centre of gravity: the actual technical problem, or a genuinely interesting thing they have shipped or published. Credibility is earned by specificity here more than anywhere.
- Value to them: Pete knows the space, is not a keyword-matcher, and covers the whole stack so they are not passed between three recruiters.
- Be plainly a recruiter, but signal fast that he is a specialist one. That distinction is the entire reason they keep reading.
- Avoid: reciting their stack, buzzwords, marketing tone, over-explaining anything technical. Assume they know more than Pete about their own system.

## Engineering leadership (CTO, VP Eng, Head of Robotics, Eng Director)

Owns the team-build and feels the hard seats most. The difficult-role lean-in lands best with this seat.

- Centre of gravity: technical credibility plus a signal that Pete reads the hiring market for their space. They want a partner who will not waste their time.
- Value to them: the hard role gets Pete's specialist attention, and one partner covers the rest of the stack.
- Lean into the live hard role as the one Pete would start on, framed as his market read, never their struggle.
- Avoid: the pain-guessing frame, generic "scaling the team" filler.

## Talent / TA / People team

Sees more agency spam than anyone and can smell a blast instantly. Most rec-to-rec and generalist outreach does not understand robotics, so domain fluency is the differentiator.

- Centre of gravity: partnership and coverage. Still open with a specific product/company detail so it is obvious this is not a blast, then shift to why Pete is a single, low-hassle partner for the hard robotics and autonomy roles.
- Value to them: one specialist who genuinely knows the space and covers the whole stack, so they are not briefing five vendors.
- Keep the technical depth light; a TA contact does not need the middleware discussion, they need to trust the domain fit and the process.
- Avoid: deep technical deep-dive, treating them as the decision-maker on role specifics when they may be coordinating.
